About the Painting

Booker T. Washington stands among the most influential educators, authors, institution builders, and leaders in American history. Born into slavery, he rose through education, determination, and disciplined work to become the founding leader of Tuskegee Institute and one of the most recognized African American voices of his time.

In this commanding portrait, contemporary artist Kenneth J. Lewis Sr. presents Washington through a bold and expressive visual language. The work does not attempt to reproduce a formal photograph. Instead, Washington's dignity, focus, intelligence, and strength are communicated through visible brushwork, strong outlines, layered color, and richly textured oil paint.

Washington is shown in formal dress with a gray suit, white shirt, and deep red bow tie. His direct but contemplative expression creates an immediate connection with the viewer. The muted earth-toned background allows the figure to dominate the composition while reinforcing the work's historical presence.

Across the lower portion of the painting, the words “Up From Slavery” appear in expressive dripping white paint. The phrase references Washington's landmark autobiography while connecting his historic journey with the raw, contemporary character of the painting.

The contrast between the historically significant subject and the artist's modern, uninhibited application creates a work that belongs equally within conversations about American history, African American achievement, contemporary portraiture, education, transformation, and cultural memory.

The Significance of the Surface

This painting was created on reclaimed discarded plywood rather than a conventional stretched canvas. The surface is not simply a support for the image. It is an essential part of the meaning of the work.

The grain, dents, scars, weathering, seams, and imperfections of the wood remain visible beneath and around the paint. These marks preserve the history of the material and allow the surface to participate in the portrait. What had been considered damaged, ordinary, or disposable has been transformed into an important original work of art.

That transformation reflects the larger message associated with Booker T. Washington's life. His story demonstrates that a person's beginnings do not determine the limits of their achievement. Through education, discipline, skill, purpose, and perseverance, something that others may overlook can reveal extraordinary value.

Kenneth J. Lewis Sr. does not conceal the previous life of the plywood. He allows its imperfections to remain as evidence of endurance. The material and the subject therefore become inseparable: both communicate resilience, transformation, and the power to rise beyond imposed limitations.

Artistic significance: The reclaimed surface gives the painting a physical and conceptual presence that cannot be duplicated on a manufactured canvas. Every irregularity in the wood makes this work entirely singular.
Kenneth J. Lewis Sr.'s Artistic Approach

Kenneth J. Lewis Sr. is a self-taught contemporary artist whose work is recognized for its expressive brushwork, direct mark making, visible texture, strong drawing, and emotional immediacy. His paintings preserve the evidence of their creation. Brushstrokes are not polished away, and the surface is not made mechanically perfect.

In this portrait, thick and thin passages of oil paint work together with the natural character of the plywood. The face is developed through warm ochres, browns, golds, muted reds, and darker linear marks. Each stroke contributes to the structure of the portrait while retaining the artist's distinctive hand.

The result is a painting that can be experienced from across a large room and studied closely for its individual marks, surface variations, and layers of color. It is both historically grounded and unmistakably contemporary.

A Work for Important Collections

This painting is designed to hold significant visual presence in a museum, gallery, university, school, library, cultural center, corporate collection, foundation, government building, historical institution, private residence, or serious personal collection.

At 48 × 60 inches, the work is large enough to serve as a focal point without overwhelming an interior. Its subject, scale, and material history give it the ability to anchor an educational exhibition, executive office, reading room, institutional corridor, conference room, cultural center, library, or thoughtfully designed home.

For collectors and art dealers, the painting offers a distinctive combination of recognizable historical significance, contemporary expression, unconventional material, and one-of-a-kind surface character. For institutions, it provides an opportunity to acquire a work capable of supporting conversations about education, leadership, history, resilience, craftsmanship, and cultural progress.
